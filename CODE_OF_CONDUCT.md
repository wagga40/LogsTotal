# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are talking to is acting in good faith, is
better informed than you about some things and worse about others, and is spending their
own time here.

## What is expected

- **Argue about the work, not the person.** "This breaks X" is a review. "You clearly did
  not read X" is not.
- **Take criticism of your code as criticism of your code.** Anyone can be wrong about a
  patch, including the maintainer, including you.
- **Say what you actually think, plainly.** Politeness is not vagueness. A clear "no, and
  here is why" is kinder than an ambiguous maybe.
- **Respect the answer once it is given.** Several things in LogsTotal that look like
  omissions are deliberate and documented as such; if a decision has been explained,
  re-litigating it in a new thread is not a new argument.

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning comments about anyone or any group, unwelcome
attention, publishing someone's private information, and deliberate disruption. These get
you removed, with or without a warning depending on severity.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else the project is
being discussed on behalf of the project.

## Reporting

Report it privately to the maintainer ([@wagga40](https://github.com/wagga40)) through the
private reporting form linked from [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), and say it is a conduct report.
Reports are handled privately. Say what happened and where; you do not need to make a case, and you will not
be asked to confront the person yourself.

The maintainer decides the response — anything from a private word to a permanent ban —
and is bound by the same rules as everyone else.
